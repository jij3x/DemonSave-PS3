## Character Classes

In Demon's Souls, the starting class is referred to in character creation as an **"Origin."** The Origin determines three things:

1. The initial attribute allocations (**starting stats**)
2. The **starting Soul Level**
3. The **initial equipment**

## Stat Effects on the Character

This section details what each attribute affects, sourced from the stat pages linked by the classes table.

### How Stats Are Upgraded

In order to level stats in a new game, you must:
1. Beat the boss of 1-1
2. Talk to **The Monumental**
3. Then you can upgrade stats with **The Maiden in Black**

Each level-up grants **1 point** for allocation by the player into a chosen stat.

### General Stat Rules (from /stats)

- **No speed stats exist.** There are no stat increases for weapon speed, attack speed, decreased falldown duration, attack delay, or anything else of that nature.
- **If an item is not present in the stat tables, increasing any stat will not help** with that item.
- **Every stat increases physical defense** — no matter how stat points are spent, less damage will be taken.
- **Increasing Endurance causes a greater increase in Fire resistance** than any other stat.
- **Maximum single stat:** 99.
- **Maximum Soul Level:** 712 (all individual stats at 99).
- The maximum is far beyond what's needed to complete ~3.5 playthroughs and earn the Platinum trophy.
- **De-levelling:** The only way to de-level stats is to decrease the overall Soul Level, then build back the desired stats. See the `/delevel` page.

### Vitality (Vit)

**General Effects:**
- Increases **Max HP**
- Increases **Carry Weight** (max item load)
- **Not** a weapon requirement

**Notes:**
- Max HP in Soul Form can be increased by wearing the **Cling Ring**.
- Carry Weight can also be increased by wearing the **Ring of Herculean Strength**.
- **Falling damage** is affected by Vitality, because it is calculated as a percentage of your current full HP rather than a fixed amount.

### Intelligence (Int)

### Endurance (End)

**General Effects:**
- Increases **Max Stamina**
- Increases **Equip Burden** (max equipment weight)
- Increases **Fire resistance**
- Increases **Poison resistance**
- Increases **Bleed resistance**
- **Not** a weapon requirement

**Notes:**
- Items that count against Equip Burden: head, body, arm, and leg armor, plus any weapon/shield in the four hand slots. Ammunition, consumables, and rings do **not** count against Equip Burden (but do count against Item Burden).
- **Roll speed thresholds:**
  - Equipment ≤ 50% of max Equip Burden → full-speed roll
  - 50%–100% → slow roll (controller rumbles on landing)
  - Exceeds 100% → can only walk slowly; rolling/backstepping leaves you stunned
- **Max Stamina is reached at 40 Endurance** — raising Endurance beyond 40 does not increase Stamina further.
- Higher Endurance = more attacks in a combo before needing to rest.

### Luck

**General Effects:**
- Increases **Item drop rate**
- Increases **Disease (plague) resistance**
- **Not** a weapon requirement

**Notes:**
- Luck does **not** increase the quality of item drops — only **how often** enemies drop items.
- Disease resistance can be further increased by wearing the **Ring of Disease Resistance** or wielding **Bramd** or **Istarelle**.
- Although Luck is not a weapon requirement, it affects both the **Physical and Magical damage of the Blueblood Sword**.

### Soul Level (SL)

**General Effects:**
- Increases **Soul points required for next Soul Level**
- Increases **Physical, Magic, and Fire Defense**
- Determines the **level range** from which Blue Phantoms and Black Phantoms can be summoned
- Grants **1 point** for allocation into a chosen stat upon each level up

**Notes:**
- From SL 1 to SL 120 costs **3,506,802 total souls**.
- The soul cost increases in a pattern that repeats every 25 levels, with minor adjustments around SL 260 and SL 711.
- Soul cost can be modeled by: `y = 0.02x³ + 3.06x² + 105.6x − 895` (accurate from level 12 and up), where `x` = level and `y` = souls required.
- The table values are **per-level requirements**, not cumulative.

**Exact Values (sample, per-level soul cost):**

|:----------:|:------------:|:---------:|:--------:|:--------------------:|
| 1          | 12           | 16        | 10       | 673                  |
| 5          | 18           | 21        | 15       | 741                  |
| 10         | 25           | 27        | 22       | 829                  |
| 100        | —            | —         | —        | ~69,000              |
| 712        | —            | —         | —        | Max                  |

## Notes (Classes Page)

The wiki classes page includes the following guidance and mechanics notes.

### Classes Are Not a Straitjacket

The starting class is **not** a permanent role choice. The term used in character creation is **"Origin,"** and it only determines:

- The initial attribute allocations (starting stats)
- The starting Soul Level
- The initial equipment

You can eventually upgrade your stats to use **any type of weapon, armor, or magic**, though this requires many hours of play. Players are advised to pick a class whose stats and equipment naturally lean toward their preferred early playstyle, then upgrade stats to support that role.

### Maximum Soul Level (712) Convergence

All classes reach the **exact same stats** and can do the exact same things at the maximum Soul Level of **712**. The starting class therefore has no effect on a character's maximum potential.

Maxing out stats does not give any unfair advantage over other players because of the game's online Soul Level range restrictions.

### Delevelling and Soul Suck

If a player **loses a level** (for example, when "soulsucked" in PvP or PvE), the class determines the **order in which stats are lost** each time a level is removed.

- The practical gameplay difference between classes is described as **extremely minor**.
- Example given: a Lv712 **Knight** will first lose **Luck**, while a **Magician** will first lose **Faith**.

The wiki links to the `/delevel` (delevelling) page for more details.

### Soul Cost of Leveling by Origin

Because each Origin starts with 80 stat points plus its Soul Level in extra pre-allocated increases:

- **Royalty** starts with **81 total stat points** at **Soul Level 1**, giving it the fewest total stats but the **minimum soul cost** to increase them.
- A **Knight** starts with **84 total stat points**.
- A **Thief** starts with **89 total stat points** at **Soul Level 9**. Starting as a Thief means raising a stat by one point costs **about four times as much** as it does for the Royalty origin.

In other words, lower-Soul-Level origins (especially Royalty) are more soul-efficient for long-term builds, while higher-Soul-Level origins begin the game with more total stats but pay a higher soul cost for every subsequent increase.
